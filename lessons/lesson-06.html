Warm-up A · Grammar

Name the tense

Five tenses, ten sentences. Which tense is the bold part? Say why out loud — find the time word.

All five tenses

Two questions choose the tense: WHEN is it (now, in general, in the past, in your life)? And is it a finished event or something in progress?

Present Simple

I play · she plays · Does she play?

Habits, routines, facts. every day · usually · never · on Mondays

She plays tennis every Saturday.

Present Continuous

I'm playing · she's playing · Is she playing?

Happening now or around now; fixed plans. now · at the moment · today · this week

She's playing tennis right now.

Past Simple

I played / went · she didn't play · Did she play?

A finished event at a finished time. yesterday · last week · ago · in 2020

She played tennis yesterday.

Past Continuous

I was playing · they were playing · Were they playing?

In progress at a moment in the past; the background of a story. while · at 8 pm yesterday

She was playing tennis when it started to rain.

Present Perfect

I've played · she has played · Has she ever played?

Experience in your life, time not said. ever · never · so far · twice

She has played tennis at Wimbledon.

Watch out

I am go to school every day.I go to school every day.
Look! It snows.Look! It's snowing.
I have seen him yesterday.I saw him yesterday.
While I walked home, it started to rain.While I was walking home, it started to rain.
Did you ever eat snails?Have you ever eaten snails?

Exercise 3 · Grammar

Choose the right form

The trickiest pairs from Lessons 3–5: Simple or Continuous? Past Simple or Present Perfect?

Present Perfect vs Past Simple

No time → Present Perfect. A finished time (yesterday, last summer, in 2020, when I was ten) → Past Simple.

The one question to ask

no time said → Present Perfect · finished time → Past Simple

If you can answer 'When?' with a finished time, it's the Past Simple.

I've been to Lviv.

I went to Lviv last summer.

The conversation pattern

Have you ever…? → Yes, I have. → When / Where / Who / How did you…?

Open with the Present Perfect, then switch to the Past Simple for the details.

Have you ever broken a bone? — Yes, I have. I broke my arm when I was nine.

Time words

PP: ever · never · before · so far · this year · twice PS: yesterday · last week · in 2019 · ago · when I was…

'this year' / 'today' are not finished yet → Present Perfect. Questions with When? always take the Past Simple.

She's won three medals so far.

When did you start skateboarding?

Watch out

I have seen that film last week.I saw that film last week.
When have you started school?When did you start school?
Did you ever try snails?Have you ever tried snails?

Part 2 · new

Comparatives and superlatives

Two things → comparative + than. The top one of a group → the + superlative.

cheap → cheaper → the cheapest · big → bigger → the biggest · easy → easier → the easiest · expensive → more expensive → the most expensive · good → better → the best · bad → worse → the worst

The School Park is cheaper than Central. Central is the most expensive park in town. It's the best pizza I've ever had.

Exercise 6 · Grammar · new

Make the forms

Write the comparative and the superlative. The first one is done for you.

Comparatives and superlatives

Compare two things with -er / more … than. Pick the top one of a group with the -est / the most.

Short adjectives (one syllable)

cheap → cheaper → the cheapest nice → nicer → the nicest big → bigger → the biggest

Add -er / -est. Ends in -e: just -r / -st. One vowel + one consonant: double it (big, hot, thin, sad).

My room is bigger than yours.

It's the cheapest café in town.

Adjectives ending in -y

easy → easier → the easiest funny → funnier → the funniest

y changes to i, then -er / -est.

Maths is easier than physics for me.

Long adjectives (two or more syllables)

expensive → more expensive → the most expensive

Use more / the most — and never add -er as well.

This phone is more expensive than that one.

It was the most boring film of the year.

Irregular

good → better → the best bad → worse → the worst far → further → the furthest

Learn these three by heart — they come up all the time.

It's the best pizza I've ever had.

than, the … in / of, the … ever

A is bigger than B · the tallest in the class · the best of all · the best I've ever …

than after a comparative; in + a place or group; superlative + Present Perfect with ever.

He's the tallest boy in our class.

much / a bit + comparative

much cheaper · a bit taller · far more interesting

much / far = a big difference; a bit = a small difference. Not very cheaper!

The train is much faster than the bus.

Watch out

more biggerbigger
the most tallestthe tallest
bigger then mebigger than me
gooder / the goodestbetter / the best
the most expensive of the citythe most expensive in the city
very cheapermuch cheaper

Exercise 10 · Reading

Three parks, one winner?

Read Max's review and answer: which park?

Our town has three skate parks, and my friends and I have skated at all of them, so here's my honest review.

Riverside Park is the oldest. It opened in 2009 and it looks really old-fashioned now — the ramps are cracked, and in winter it's the coldest place in town because of the wind from the river. But it's free, and it's never crowded, so it's the most peaceful of the three.

Central Skate Zone is the newest and the most modern. The ramps are huge, and the bowl is much bigger than the one at Riverside. The problem is the price: an hour costs 80 hryvnias, so it's by far the most expensive. And on Saturdays it's so crowded that you wait ten minutes for every run.

The School Park is right behind my school, so for me it's the most convenient. It's smaller than Central, but it's safer than Riverside because the surface is new. It costs 30 hryvnias — cheaper than Central, but obviously not as cheap as Riverside.

So which one is the best? For learning new tricks, Central, no question. For hanging out with friends after lessons, the School Park. And when I just want to skate alone and think? Riverside. I've skated at all three hundreds of times, and I still can't choose just one.

— Max, 14

Exercise 13 · GrammarExtra

as … as

Same meaning, different words. Finish the second sentence.

as … as · not as … as

as + adjective + as = the same. not as … as = less.

The same

as + adjective + as

Two things are equal.

My brother is as tall as my dad now.

Less

not as + adjective + as

The first thing is less. Often a polite way to compare.

The bus isn't as fast as the train.

Same meaning, two ways

The bus is slower than the train. = The bus isn't as fast as the train.

Good for exam transformations.

Pietro's isn't as tasty as La Bella Napoli.

Watch out

as tall thanas tall as
not as taller asnot as tall as

03 · нове

than, the … in, the … I've ever

Два предмети → вищий ступінь + than
The metro is faster than the bus.
Найкращий із групи → the + найвищий ступінь + in / of
He's the tallest boy in our class.

than ≠ then. than — «ніж», then — «потім». Звучать майже однаково, але пишуться по-різному.

in + місце чи група (in the city, in the team, in my family); of + період чи «усі» (of the year, of all).

It's the best pizza I've ever had.

Найвищий ступінь дуже часто йде разом із Present Perfect + ever — «найкраще з усього, що я будь-коли пробував».
