Lesson 17 — Happy Accidents

A dirty dish, a melted chocolate bar, a walk with a dog — some of the greatest inventions in history happened by accident. If those people had been more careful, we wouldn't have them today! Today: the last kind of “if” — the third conditional, about the past that can't change. Plus a mysterious old clock that gives you a second chance…

Part 1 · new grammar

The third conditional

0 Zero

If + Present, Present

Always true — facts, science.

If you heat ice, it melts.

1 First

If + Present, will + V

Real future — it can happen.

If it snows, we'll go skiing.

2 Second

If + Past, would + V

Imaginary now — dreams, advice.

If I had wings, I'd fly.

3 Third new

If + had + V3, would have + V3

Imaginary past — it didn't happen; regrets, lucky accidents.

If I had left earlier, I wouldn't have missed the bus.

Third conditional = the past that can't change. If I had studied, I would have passed. → In reality: I didn't study, and I didn't pass. Ukrainian: «якби я (тоді) вчився, я б склав».

Form: If + had + V3, would have + V3. Short forms: I'd have passed, I wouldn't have missed. Negative: If I hadn't forgotten…

Never would after if: ✗ If I would have known → ✓ If I had known. And always V3: ✗ would have went → ✓ would have gone.

Exercise 2 · Grammar · new

Regrets

What really happened → what would have happened. Careful: positive in real life becomes negative in the conditional, and the other way round!

  • I didn't charge my phone, so it died.
    → If I my phone, it .
  • She forgot her umbrella and got wet.
    → If she her umbrella, she wet.
  • We didn't book tickets, so we didn't see the show.
    → If we tickets, we the show.
  • He ate too much cake and felt sick.
    → If he so much cake, he sick.
  • I didn't know it was your birthday, so I didn't buy a present.
    → If I it was your birthday, I a present.

Exercise 3 · Reading

Four lucky mistakes

Read the four true stories. Then do the tasks — and say which invention you use most.

The mould that saved millions

In September 1928, the Scottish scientist Alexander Fleming came back from a summer holiday to his messy laboratory in London. Before leaving, he hadn't cleaned some of his glass dishes with bacteria. On one of them, a strange blue-green mould was growing — and around it, the bacteria had died. Fleming had discovered penicillin, the first antibiotic. Since then it has saved millions of lives, and in 1945 Fleming won the Nobel Prize. If he had been a tidier scientist, he would never have noticed it.

The chocolate in his pocket

In 1945, the American engineer Percy Spencer was working on radar equipment when he noticed something strange: the chocolate bar in his pocket had melted. He was curious, so he put some popcorn next to the machine — and it popped all over the lab! Spencer realised that the waves from the radar could heat food. A few years later, the first microwave oven was sold. It was almost two metres tall!

The dog and the burrs

In 1941, the Swiss engineer George de Mestral went for a walk in the mountains with his dog. When they got home, both his trousers and the dog's fur were covered in burrs — small seed balls from a plant. He looked at them under a microscope and saw thousands of tiny hooks. That gave him an idea: two strips of fabric, one with hooks and one with loops. It took him years, but the result was Velcro.

The glue that didn't work

In 1968, Spencer Silver, a scientist at the company 3M, was trying to make a very strong glue. Instead, he made a very weak one: it stuck to things, but you could easily pull it off again. For years nobody knew what to do with it. Then his colleague Art Fry, who sang in a church choir, got annoyed because his paper bookmarks kept falling out of his song book. He put some of Silver's glue on them — and the Post-it note was born.

02 · third conditional

Якби тоді… то б…

If I had studied, I would have passed. → насправді: не вчився і не склав.
If she hadn't missed the bus, she wouldn't have met him. → насправді: спізнилась — і познайомилась.
If Fleming had cleaned his dishes, he wouldn't have discovered penicillin.

Реальність і умова — навпаки: якщо в реальності «так», в умові «ні», і навпаки: I didn't charge my phone → If I had charged it…

Скорочено: I'd have passed · I wouldn't have missed. А I'd після if — це I had: If I'd known…
