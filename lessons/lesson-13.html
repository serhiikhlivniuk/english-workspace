Part 1 · new grammar

who, which, where, whose

Two sentences → one. He's a striker. He scores with both feet. → He's a striker who scores with both feet.

who — people · which — things and animals · that — people or things (informal) · where — places · whose — «чий»: a player whose father was a footballer.

Don't repeat the person or thing: ✗ a striker who he scores → ✓ a striker who scores · ✗ the town where he was born there → ✓ the town where he was born

Exercise 5 · Reading

An email from the chief scout

Sam, the club's chief scout, sends you three names. Read the email, then answer.

From: Sam Okafor, chief scout
To: The Gaffer
Subject: Three names for the transfer window

Boss,

The transfer window closes on Friday, and we've still got £30 million to spend. We need a striker who can score fifteen goals a season, and a midfielder who can control the game. I've watched more than forty players this autumn. Here are the three I like most.

1. Tomás Ruiz, 23, striker, plays in Portugal. Tomás is a player who scores with both feet and with his head — eighteen goals last season. He's fast, confident and very ambitious. The problem? His agent, whose demands are always high, wants a transfer fee of £20 million and huge wages. And Tomás can be a bit arrogant: in March he was sent off for arguing with the referee.

2. Kofi Mensah, 19, midfielder, from Ghana. Kofi is playing on loan at a Dutch club where he has become a fan favourite. His passing is the best I've seen at his age, and he's the most hard-working player in the squad. He's quite shy and his English isn't great yet, so he'll need time. His club would accept a bid of £9 million.

3. Danylo Kravets, 27, midfielder, plays in Poland. Danylo is a player who never complains and never hides. He's the captain of a team which won the league last season, and every coach I spoke to called him reliable and calm. Last year he had a knee injury, but he got over it quickly and hasn't missed a match since. His contract ends next summer, so we could sign him for £11 million.

My advice: Tomás is the one who will sell shirts, but Kofi and Danylo are the ones who will win matches. It's your decision, boss.

Sam

1. Which player…

  • Which player is the most expensive?
  • Which player is the youngest?
  • Which player is playing for a club that doesn't own him?
  • Which player had an injury last year?
  • Which player was sent off for arguing?
  • Which player is a captain?
  • Which player needs time to learn the language?

2. True or false?

  • The club has got £30 million to spend.
  • The club needs a goalkeeper and a striker.
  • Tomás's agent is easy to work with.
  • Kofi's club would sell him for less than £10 million.
  • Danylo has missed many matches since his injury.
  • Sam thinks Tomás will win the most matches.

Exercise 6 · Speaking · role-play

Deadline day

Three rounds — and you have £30 million. Use who, which, where, whose every time you describe a player.

  1. The decision (2 min). Which two players do you want, and why? “I want Kofi, who…, because we need a player whose…” I'll be the club owner — and I'll disagree with you!
  2. The negotiation (5 min). I'm Tomás's agent. You want him for £15 million; I want £20 million and big wages. Try to get a deal. Useful: That's too much. · Our final offer is… · What if we…? · Take it or leave it. · It's a deal!
  3. The press conference (3 min). You've signed your new player. I'm a journalist (remember Lesson 9!): Why did you choose him? What kind of player is he? Who did you compare him with?

Bonus: describe a player from your own team or class to me like a scout: He's a defender who… He's the kind of player whose…

Граматика уроку

Підрядні означальні речення — спосіб з'єднати два короткі речення в одне довге і пояснити, про кого чи про що мова. Українською: «гравець, який забиває обома ногами», «клуб, чиї вболівальники…».

01 · слова-зв'язки

who · which · that · where · whose

словодля чогоприклад
whoлюди — «який, яка, які»a scout who finds new players
whichречі, твариниthe contract which he signed
thatлюди або речі (у розмові)a player that never gives up · the boots that I bought
whereмісця — «де»the town where he was born
whose«чий, чия, чиї»a player whose father was a footballer
02 · як з'єднати

Два речення → одне

He's a striker. He scores with both feet. → He's a striker who scores with both feet.
This is the town. Haaland was born here. → This is the town where Haaland was born.
I met a girl. Her father is a referee. → I met a girl whose father is a referee.

Слово-зв'язка замінює he / she / it / here / her — тому їх більше не повторюємо: ✗ a striker who he scores → ✓ a striker who scores.

03 · можна пропустити

the boots I bought

Якщо після who / which / that іде інша особа (I, you, he…), слово-зв'язку в розмові часто пропускають:

Можна пропустити
the boots (which) I bought
the player (who) we signed
Не можна пропустити — коли воно саме «робить» дію
a player who scores a lot
✗ a player scores a lot
04 · пастки

Типові помилки

who's ≠ whosewho's = who is: Who's that? · whose = чий: a boy whose dad is a coach. ✗ a boy who's dad
which — не для людей✗ the player which scored → ✓ the player who scored
where — лише для місць з повним реченнямthe house where I grew up (=in which) · але the house which I bought (купив саме будинок, не «в ньому»).
05 · бонус

З комами — додаткова інформація

Якщо речення вже зрозуміле і ми просто додаємо факт, ставимо коми і не вживаємо that: Haaland, who was born in Leeds, plays for Manchester City. · The agent, whose name I always forget, called again.
