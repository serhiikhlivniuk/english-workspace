Lesson 10 — What's He Like?

Three tiny words — a, an, the — and sometimes no word at all. Ukrainian doesn't have them, so they're the most common mistake Ukrainians make in English. Today we sort them out on a trip to London — the Thames, the Tube, the London Eye and a Chelsea match at Stamford Bridge — with two short BBC videos. Then a new topic: personality. You already know lots of these words — talented, stubborn, calm, determined, tough — now we'll add more and use them to describe the people you meet on a trip, in a team or at home.

Part 1 · grammar

a, an, the — or nothing

a / an = one of many, or the first time we mention it: Dad bought a ticket. I saw a fox in the park. · a before a consonant sound, an before a vowel sound: a girl, an honest girl, a university, an hour.

the = we both know which one: there's only one, we've mentioned it before, or it's the best/worst: The ticket was for the Chelsea match. the Tube, the Thames, the best seat, the Champions League, the UK.

no article = in general, sports, most countries and cities, some places: English fans sing a lot. I play football. We flew to London. Chelsea play at Stamford Bridge.

Why “the” / why nothing?

the Tower of London, the River Thames, the London Eye, the UK — famous buildings, rivers and countries with Kingdom / States. But (—) London, (—) Ukraine, (—) Stamford Bridge, (—) Heathrow — most cities, countries, stadiums and airports have no article.

Part 2 · new

What's he like?

Three questions — three different answers.

What's he like? — personality: He's kind and a bit shy. · What does he look like? — appearance: He's tall with dark hair. · What does he like? — things he enjoys: He likes football.

Adjective alone or with a noun? He's honest. → He's an honest person. · She's patient. → She's a really patient teacher. · He's the most honest person I know.

Exercise 5 · Grammar + Speaking

like, look like or like?

First choose the question for each answer. Then we talk.

  • — “He's tall, with short dark hair.”
  • — “He's really kind and a bit shy.”
  • — “He likes cooking and old films.”
  • — “She's friendly and very reliable.”
  • — “She's got long red hair and glasses.”
  • — “She loves reading and playing the guitar.”

Now describe…

  1. your best friend — three words for his personality, and an example for each: He's a really reliable friend — he's never late.
  2. a teacher or a neighbour — What's she like? What does she look like?
  3. someone in your family you take after — I take after my dad: we're both stubborn.
  4. yourself — one good quality and one not-so-good one. Be honest!

Game — Guess who: describe a famous person's personality — a footballer, an actor, a singer, a cartoon character — in three sentences, without saying what they do. I guess. Then swap. Use a / an / the: She's an ambitious woman. She's the most … person I can think of.

A weekend in London

Exercise 6 · Reading

Dima's trip to Stamford Bridge

Dima writes about his first trip to London and a Chelsea match. Read about the people he met, then answer.

Last month my dad took me to London for my birthday. It was my first trip to the UK, and the plan was simple: a day of sightseeing and a Chelsea match on Sunday.

We landed at Heathrow on Saturday morning and took the Tube to our hotel. The hotel was small, but the man at the reception desk was really cheerful — he gave us a map and showed us the best places to eat. In the afternoon we walked along the Thames, saw the London Eye and had fish and chips in a tiny café near the river. My dad is usually a very patient person, but not when he's hungry!

On Sunday we went to Stamford Bridge, Chelsea's stadium in west London. The nearest station is Fulham Broadway, and the train was full of fans in blue shirts. Next to us in the stand sat an old man called George. He has supported Chelsea for sixty years, and he saw the team win the Champions League twice. He was the most generous person I met in London: when he found out it was my birthday, he gave me his old Chelsea scarf!

Behind us there was a very different fan. He was loud, bad-tempered and a bit arrogant — he shouted at the referee for the whole game and complained about every pass. George just laughed. "He's been like that since 1995," he said.

The match itself was amazing. Chelsea were losing 1–0 at half-time, but they didn't give up. They scored twice in the second half, and the whole stadium exploded. I've never heard a noise like that.

On the way home I asked my dad what the best part of the trip was. "The match, of course," he said. For me, it was George. The players were great, but it's the people who make a place special. Next time I want to visit the British Museum — and take George's scarf back to Stamford Bridge.

— Dima, 14

Who…

  • Who gave them a map?
  • Who isn't patient when he's hungry?
  • Who has supported Chelsea for sixty years?
  • Who gave Dima a present?
  • Who complained about every pass?
  • Who thought the match was the best part of the trip?

05 · протилежності

un- · dis- · im- · ir-

un-: unkind, unreliable, unfriendly, unselfish
dis-: dishonest · im- (перед p): impatient, impolite · ir- (перед r): irresponsible

Прикметник + прийменник (як дієслова в уроці 8): proud of, kind to, good at, afraid of, interested in, jealous of, famous for, nervous about.
