Present Simple vs Present Continuous

The core question is simple: is this generally true, or is it happening around now?

The one question to ask

generally true → Present Simple · happening around now → Present Continuous

Ask yourself: would this still be true next month? If yes, it is a routine and takes the Simple.

I walk to school. (every day, in general)

I'm walking to school. (right now, on my way)

Time markers give it away

Simple: always · usually · every day · on Mondays · never Continuous: now · right now · at the moment · today · this week

Find the time expression first — it usually decides the tense for you.

She usually wears jeans, but today she's wearing a dress.

Both tenses in one sentence

…usually + Present Simple, but + today / at the moment + Present Continuous

This is the pattern that shows you really understand the contrast — use it in writing and speaking.

I normally have breakfast at home, but this week I'm eating at school.

My brother plays football, but he isn't playing today — he's ill.

Careful: state verbs

know · like · want · need · understand · believe — Simple only

Even when you mean right now, state verbs stay in the Simple. See the State verbs topic.

I want to go home now. (NOT I'm wanting)

Watch out

Look! He plays the guitar.Look! He's playing the guitar.
I'm going to school every day.I go to school every day.
She is knowing the answer.She knows the answer.
What do you do right now?What are you doing right now?

Regular -ed, irregular verbs, and the one rule that fixes most mistakes: no second form after did.

Spelling rules

wait → waited — most verbs
decide → decided — verb already ends in -e, add only -d

Exercise 7 · Pronunciation

How do you say the -ed?

soundwhenexamples
/t/after a voiceless soundwalked · missed · stopped · watched · laughed
/d/after a voiced soundopened · called · arrived · realised · grabbed
/ɪd/only after /t/ or /d/ — the only group that adds a syllablewanted · started · decided · needed · landed

Sort these verbs into the three groups.

The three -ed sounds

-ed is pronounced /t/, /d/ or /ɪd/ — and only the last one adds a syllable.

/t/ after a voiceless sound

walked · missed · stopped · watched · laughed

After sounds like /k/, /s/, /p/, /tʃ/, /f/. No extra syllable.

I walked home in the rain.

/d/ after a voiced sound

opened · called · arrived · realised · grabbed

After vowels and voiced consonants. No extra syllable.

She opened the door.

/ɪd/ only after /t/ or /d/

wanted · started · decided · needed · landed

This is the only group that adds a whole syllable.

We decided to walk instead.

Watch out

walk-ed (two syllables)walked (one syllable)
want-dwanted /ˈwɒntɪd/

Exercise 11 · Reading

The day it all went wrong

I have never had a worse Monday.

It started badly. My alarm didn't go off, so at ten past seven I was still fast asleep. When I finally opened my eyes, my mum was standing in the doorway with a particular look on her face. I grabbed my clothes, skipped breakfast and rushed out.

It was freezing outside and raining hard. While I was waiting at the bus stop, a van drove through an enormous puddle and soaked me from head to foot. I stood there for a second, absolutely stunned, watching the water drip off my sleeve. The bus, meanwhile, went past without stopping.

By the time I got to school I was twenty minutes late, starving and still wet. I slipped on the corridor floor in front of half my class, which was the moment I stopped caring about anything at all. Mr Kovalenko glanced at me over his glasses and said nothing, which was somehow worse than shouting.

Then something strange happened. At lunchtime I ran into Daria, who was carrying two sandwiches because her friend wasn't in that day. She gave me one without being asked. While we were eating, she told me that she once came to school in her slippers and survived it, and I laughed for the first time that day.

By four o'clock I was exhausted and still furious with my alarm clock. But I was also, in a strange way, relieved: the worst day of the year had happened, it was over, and somebody had given me a sandwich.

True or false?

  • His alarm rang, but he ignored it.
  • He left home without eating anything.
  • A van splashed him while he was waiting for the bus.
  • The bus stopped and he got on it.
  • Daria gave him food although he didn't ask for it.
  • At the end of the day he felt nothing but anger.

+   I / he / she was waiting · you / we / they were waiting
−   I wasn't waiting · they weren't waiting
?   Were you waiting? · What were you doing?
at eight o'clock yesterdayall morningwhilewhenat that moment

Правопис -ing той самий, що й у Present Continuous: run → running, make → making, lie → lying, study → studying.

04 · головне

Як вони працюють разом

While I was walking home, it started to rain.

long action, already going on → was walking   ·   short event that cuts across it → started

while + довга дія
While I was waiting, a van soaked me.
when + коротка подія
I was waiting when a van soaked me.

06 · нагадування

Дієслова стану — і в минулому теж

Правило з уроку 3 працює й тут: стани не бувають у Continuous, ані в теперішньому, ані в минулому.

know · understand · believe · remember · want · need · like · love · hate · mean · cost · belong

✗ I was knowing the answer. → ✓ I knew the answer.
✗ She was wanting to leave. → ✓ She wanted to leave.

07 · лексика

Сильні прикметники

Слова з Вправи 1 уже містять у собі «дуже», тому very з ними не вживається.

звичайнийсильнийпідсилювач
hungrystarvingabsolutely starving
wetsoakedabsolutely soaked
coldfreezingabsolutely freezing
tiredexhaustedabsolutely exhausted
angryfuriousabsolutely furious
surprisedstunnedabsolutely stunned
Правило✗ very freezing → ✓ absolutely freezing або просто freezing.
І навпаки: ✗ absolutely cold → ✓ very cold. Звичайні прикметники беруть very, сильні — absolutely.
