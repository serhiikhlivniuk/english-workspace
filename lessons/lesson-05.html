Warm-up A · Grammar revision

Four tenses in one go

Lessons 2–4 in twelve sentences: Present Simple, Present Continuous, Past Simple or Past Continuous? Look for the time words first.

Present Simple vs Present Continuous

The core question is simple: is this generally true, or is it happening around now?

The one question to ask

generally true → Present Simple · happening around now → Present Continuous

Ask yourself: would this still be true next month? If yes, it is a routine and takes the Simple.

I walk to school. (every day, in general)

I'm walking to school. (right now, on my way)

Time markers give it away

Simple: always · usually · every day · on Mondays · never Continuous: now · right now · at the moment · today · this week

Find the time expression first — it usually decides the tense for you.

She usually wears jeans, but today she's wearing a dress.

Both tenses in one sentence

…usually + Present Simple, but + today / at the moment + Present Continuous

This is the pattern that shows you really understand the contrast — use it in writing and speaking.

I normally have breakfast at home, but this week I'm eating at school.

My brother plays football, but he isn't playing today — he's ill.

Careful: state verbs

know · like · want · need · understand · believe — Simple only

Even when you mean right now, state verbs stay in the Simple. See the State verbs topic.

I want to go home now. (NOT I'm wanting)

Watch out

Look! He plays the guitar.Look! He's playing the guitar.
I'm going to school every day.I go to school every day.
She is knowing the answer.She knows the answer.
What do you do right now?What are you doing right now?

while and when

The long action is the background; the short one cuts across it.

Background and event

was / were + -ing + Past Simple

Something was already going on when a short event happened.

While I was waiting at the bus stop, a van soaked me.

He broke his arm while he was playing basketball.

Which word goes where

while + long action · when + short event

Both orders are possible; the tense tells you which action is which.

I was reading when the lights went out.

Two long actions together

was/were + -ing + was/were + -ing

Both actions lasted and happened at the same time.

While I was cooking, my brother was playing video games.

Watch out

While I walked home, I saw an accident.While I was walking home, I saw an accident.
They was waiting for me.They were waiting for me.
I was knowing the answer.I knew the answer.

Exercise 3 · Grammar

Build the Present Perfect

Put the verb in the Present Perfect. Short forms (I've, she's, hasn't) are fine.

Present Perfect: experiences

Something has happened at some time in your life — when exactly doesn't matter.

Form

have / has + past participle (V3) I've seen · she's been · we haven't tried · Have you ever…?

he / she / it → has; I / you / we / they → have. Regular verbs: V3 = -ed (visited). Irregular: learn the third form (been, seen, eaten).

I've visited London twice.

She has never eaten sushi.

ever and never

Have you ever + V3 …? · I've never + V3 …

ever = at any time in your life (questions). never = not at any time (it is already negative).

Have you ever ridden a horse?

I've never been abroad.

Short answers

Yes, I have. · No, I haven't. · Yes, she has. · No, she hasn't.

Answer with have / has — never with the V3 and never 'Yes, I've.'

Has your dad ever climbed a mountain? — Yes, he has.

been or gone

been to = went and came back · gone to = is there now

For experiences we almost always use been.

I've been to Kyiv three times.

Max isn't here — he's gone to the shop.

Watch out

Have you ever went to Italy?Have you ever been to Italy?
She has never eat sushi.She has never eaten sushi.
My parents has visited Prague.My parents have visited Prague.
Yes, I've.Yes, I have.
I haven't never been there.I've never been there.

Exercise 6 · Grammar · the main one

Present Perfect or Past Simple?

No time said → Present Perfect. A finished time (last summer, in 2019, when?) → Past Simple. The conversation often starts with one and continues with the other.

Present Perfect vs Past Simple

No time → Present Perfect. A finished time (yesterday, last summer, in 2020, when I was ten) → Past Simple.

The one question to ask

no time said → Present Perfect · finished time → Past Simple

If you can answer 'When?' with a finished time, it's the Past Simple.

I've been to Lviv.

I went to Lviv last summer.

The conversation pattern

Have you ever…? → Yes, I have. → When / Where / Who / How did you…?

Open with the Present Perfect, then switch to the Past Simple for the details.

Have you ever broken a bone? — Yes, I have. I broke my arm when I was nine.

Time words

PP: ever · never · before · so far · this year · twice PS: yesterday · last week · in 2019 · ago · when I was…

'this year' / 'today' are not finished yet → Present Perfect. Questions with When? always take the Past Simple.

She's won three medals so far.

When did you start skateboarding?

Watch out

I have seen that film last week.I saw that film last week.
When have you started school?When did you start school?
Did you ever try snails?Have you ever tried snails?

Seven things, three ticks

Read Max's text about his bucket list. There are words from Lesson 4 hiding in it — can you spot five?

Last year my cousin showed me her bucket list — a list of things she wants to do before she's thirty. I thought it was a brilliant idea, so I made my own. It has seven things on it, and so far I've ticked off three.

First, I've climbed a real mountain. Last August my dad and I climbed Hoverla, the highest mountain in Ukraine. We started at six in the morning, and by the time we reached the top I was absolutely exhausted. Halfway up it started to rain and we got soaked, but the view from the top was unforgettable.

Second, I've slept in a tent. That happened on the same trip. It wasn't as romantic as it sounds: I woke up at three in the morning because there was an enormous puddle under my sleeping bag.

Third, I've met a famous person — well, almost famous. In March I ran into a skateboarder from a YouTube channel I watch every week. He was practising at our local skate park. I was so stunned that I couldn't say a word. Eventually I asked for a selfie, and he said yes. I was so relieved!

I haven't ticked off the other four yet. I've never been abroad, I've never ridden a horse, and I've never tried surfing. Number seven is the hardest: I want to play the guitar well enough to play one song for my family without a mistake. I've started lessons twice and I've given up twice. Maybe the third time will be lucky.

— Max, 14

04 · extra

just · already · yet

just — щойно: The bus has just left.
already — вже (раніше, ніж чекали): I’ve already seen it.
yet — ще не / вже? — лише в запереченнях і питаннях, у кінці: I haven’t finished yet. Have you finished yet?

just і already стоять між have і V3; yet — у кінці речення.
