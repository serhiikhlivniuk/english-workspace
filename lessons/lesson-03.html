play / go / do

Three verbs that go with different kinds of activity.

play

play + a ball game or a game with rules and an opponent

football, basketball, tennis, chess, video games, the guitar

They play basketball after school.

go

go + an activity ending in -ing

swimming, running, cycling, skateboarding, shopping, fishing

I go swimming twice a week.

do

do + an individual activity without a ball

yoga, karate, gymnastics, athletics, exercise, homework

She does karate on Saturdays.

Watch out

I play swimming.I go swimming.
He goes karate.He does karate.

Adverbs of frequency

Where to put always, usually, often and never — and why that position is not random.

The main rule

before the main verb, but AFTER the verb to be

always · usually · often · sometimes · hardly ever · never

I always do my homework.

He is never late.

With an auxiliary verb

auxiliary + adverb + main verb

When there is can, will, have, or am/is/are + -ing.

She can usually help me.

Frequency expressions

once a week · twice a month · every day — at the start or end of the sentence

These expressions never go in the middle of the sentence.

We train twice a week.

Watch out

I go always to school by bus.I always go to school by bus.
He never is tired.He is never tired.

Exercise 5 · GrammarToday's grammar

Every day or right now?

Present Simple or Present Continuous? Short forms are fine.

Present Simple vs Present Continuous

The core question is simple: is this generally true, or is it happening around now?

The one question to ask

generally true → Present Simple · happening around now → Present Continuous

Ask yourself: would this still be true next month? If yes, it is a routine and takes the Simple.

I walk to school. (every day, in general)

I'm walking to school. (right now, on my way)

Time markers give it away

Simple: always · usually · every day · on Mondays · never Continuous: now · right now · at the moment · today · this week

Find the time expression first — it usually decides the tense for you.

She usually wears jeans, but today she's wearing a dress.

Both tenses in one sentence

…usually + Present Simple, but + today / at the moment + Present Continuous

This is the pattern that shows you really understand the contrast — use it in writing and speaking.

I normally have breakfast at home, but this week I'm eating at school.

My brother plays football, but he isn't playing today — he's ill.

Careful: state verbs

know · like · want · need · understand · believe — Simple only

Even when you mean right now, state verbs stay in the Simple. See the State verbs topic.

I want to go home now. (NOT I'm wanting)

Watch out

Look! He plays the guitar.Look! He's playing the guitar.
I'm going to school every day.I go to school every day.
She is knowing the answer.She knows the answer.
What do you do right now?What are you doing right now?

Exercise 6 · Grammar

State verbs

Some verbs describe a state, not an action — you can't film them, so they don't take the Continuous. Choose the correct sentence.

State verbs

Verbs that describe a state, not an action. They are not used in the Continuous.

What they are

like · love · hate · want · need · prefer · know · understand · believe · remember · mean · cost · belong · seem

These verbs describe a state you cannot film, so they stay in the Simple even when we talk about right now.

I know the answer.

Do you need any help?

Verbs that work both ways

have · think · see · taste

When the verb describes an action rather than a state, the Continuous is fine.

I have a bike. / I'm having breakfast.

I think you're right. / I'm thinking about it.

Watch out

I am knowing the answer.I know the answer.
Are you needing help?Do you need help?
She is wanting a new phone.She wants a new phone.

Exercise 8 · Reading

Sofiia's two days

My name's Sofiia and I'm fourteen. On a normal school day my alarm goes off at half past six, and I hate it. I get up at twenty to seven, have a shower, and eat exactly the same breakfast every single morning: toast and tea. I leave the house at ten past eight and catch the number 12 bus with my friend Marta. Lessons finish at half past two. After that I usually go to the pool — I swim four times a week — and I do my homework between eight and ten in the evening. I never get to bed before midnight, which is exactly why I hate my alarm.

But today is different. It's Tuesday, and I'm sitting at home in my pyjamas at eleven o'clock in the morning. The heating at school isn't working, so they've sent everybody home. Marta is coming round at one, we're making pancakes, and nobody is doing any homework. Outside it's raining hard. It's the best Tuesday of the year.

True or false?

  • Sofiia gets out of bed as soon as her alarm goes off.
  • She has something different for breakfast every day.
  • She goes swimming more than three times a week.
  • Today she is at school as usual.
  • Marta is going to visit her this afternoon.
  • The weather today is sunny.

Find four verbs in the Present Continuous in the second paragraph. Why does Sofiia switch tense there? Which paragraph would you use to describe your own Tuesday?

Exercise 12 · GrammarExtra

Plans in your diary

We use the Present Continuous for arrangements — things already agreed with someone. Timetables stay in the Present Simple.

Present Continuous for plans

Arrangements you have already agreed with someone use the Present Continuous.

Arrangements

am / is / are + -ing + future time expression

The plan is already agreed and, in effect, in your diary.

I'm meeting Marta at six tomorrow.

My cousin is coming to stay next week.

Timetables stay Simple

Present Simple

Buses, trains, lessons and films run on a timetable, not on your agreement.

The train leaves at 7:45.

Watch out

I meet Marta tomorrow at six.I'm meeting Marta tomorrow at six.
The train is leaving at 7:45 every day.The train leaves at 7:45.

How long does it take?

A fixed pattern for talking about how much time something needs.

The pattern

How long does it take (you) to + infinitive? / It takes (me) + time + to + infinitive.

Asking and saying how much time an activity needs.

The same pattern in the negative, and in the Continuous for something happening now.
