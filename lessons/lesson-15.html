Part 1 · grammar

Can you count it?

Countable — you can say one, two, three: a coconut → two coconuts, a fish → three fish, a berry → berries. Uncountable — no a, no -s: water, food, fruit, meat, energy, advice, information, money, news.

some — in positive sentences: We've got some water. · any — in questions and negatives: Have we got any food? We haven't got any matches.

many + countable (many coconuts) · much + uncountable (much water) — mostly in questions and negatives. a lot of — with both. a few coconuts · a little water.

Exercise 4 · Reading

Review: Cast Away

Read the review. Then do the tasks — and talk: do you agree with the reviewer? How many stars would you give the film?

Cast Away (2000) · ★★★★☆
Directed by Robert Zemeckis · Starring Tom Hanks, Helen Hunt

Some films need explosions, car chases and a hundred actors. Cast Away needs one man, one island and a volleyball — and it is one of the most gripping films of its time.

Chuck Noland (Tom Hanks) is a FedEx manager who is obsessed with time. He lives by the clock, rushes around the world and never stops checking his watch. Then, on a stormy night over the Pacific, his cargo plane crashes into the ocean. Chuck is the only survivor. He is washed ashore on a tiny island where there are no people, no phone signal and no way out.

The middle of the film is where it becomes truly special. For more than an hour there is almost no dialogue and no music — only the sound of the waves, the wind and Chuck's breathing. We watch an ordinary office worker learn, slowly and painfully, how to stay alive. He breaks open coconuts, catches fish with a home-made spear and spends hours trying to make a fire. When he finally succeeds, he dances around it and shouts, "I have made fire!" — one of the most joyful scenes in the film. Even the packages that are washed up from the crash become tools: a pair of ice skates turns into a knife — and, in one scene that is hard to watch, into a very painful way to get rid of a bad tooth.

But the hardest thing on the island isn't hunger. It's loneliness. Chuck paints a face on a volleyball, calls it Wilson and starts talking to it. It sounds silly, but by the end of the film Wilson feels like a real character, and a lot of people cry when Chuck loses him.

Four years later, Chuck builds a raft, uses part of a plastic toilet as a sail and escapes. The last part of the film, back in the "normal" world, is quieter and sadder: not everything has waited for him.

Tom Hanks gives an outstanding performance. He lost a lot of weight for the role, and filming was stopped for a year so that he could look thin and wild in the island scenes. He was nominated for an Oscar, and it's easy to see why: he keeps us interested for more than two hours almost completely on his own.

Is the film perfect? Not quite. The beginning is a little slow, and some viewers find the ending too long. But Cast Away is a film you don't forget. It makes you ask a simple question: if you lost everything, what would you keep fighting for?

Verdict: a gripping survival story and a masterclass in acting. Watch it — but maybe not on a plane.

Exercise 6 · Speaking

Would you survive?

Talk about the film and about yourself. Use if, must / have to / should and words from the review.

  1. Have you seen Cast Away? What do you remember best? If you haven't — would you like to watch it after this review?
  2. Chuck talks to a volleyball. Is that crazy — or clever? What would you talk to?
  3. Which is harder on an island: hunger, loneliness or boredom? Why?
  4. Chuck is obsessed with time at the start. What are people your age obsessed with?
  5. Desert island five: one film, one song, one book or game, one person and one object. Explain each: “I'd take my phone, but there's no signal — so I'll take…”

Try a harder if: If I were Chuck, I'd build the raft much earlier. — imaginary situation: were / had + would. Just a taste for now!

Exercise 7 · WritingFrom Lesson 14Homework

Cast Away — in your words

Now you know the story — and lots of new words. Write 100–130 words: what is the film about, and what did Chuck do to survive? You can start in class and finish at home.

Answer these questions in your text:

  1. Who is Chuck, and how does he end up on the island?
  2. What problems does he have at first — water, food, fire, shelter, injuries?
  3. What does he do to survive? (the coconuts, the fire, the fish, the FedEx boxes, the cave…)
  4. Who is Wilson, and why is he so important for Chuck?
  5. How does Chuck finally leave the island?
  6. Your opinion: would you survive? Use if.

Checklist: Past Simple for the story · two sentences with if · one rule with must / have to · one relative clause · one passive (he was washed ashore…) · two uncountable nouns (water, food, advice…) · three words from the review · capital I!

01 · злічувані чи ні

Countable vs uncountable

Злічувані — можна порахувати
a coconut → two coconuts
a berry → berries
a tool, a spear, a wave, a day
Незлічувані — без a і без -s
water, food, fruit, meat, rice

Пастка для українцівadvice, information, furniture, news — в українській можна «поради, меблі, новини», а в англійській ці слова незлічувані: ✗ advices, informations → ✓ some advice, a piece of advice.
