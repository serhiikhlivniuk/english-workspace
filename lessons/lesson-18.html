Lesson 18 — The Case of the Silver Owl

Last night something very valuable disappeared from the town museum, and the police need you. Today you're a detective: you read the true story of the most famous art theft in history, collect clues, question four suspects — and name the thief. A detective thinks in if: If the thief had come through the window, there would have been footprints. But first — a quick check of a, an, the.

Part 1 · grammar review

Four kinds of “if” — a detective's toolkit

0 Facts

If + Present, Present

If it rains, the ground gets muddy.

1 Plans

If + Present, will + V

If we check the camera, we'll know the time.

2 Guesses

If + Past, would + V

If I were the thief, I'd use the back door.

3 Conclusions

If + had V3, would have V3

If he had come in, the dog would have barked.

Part 2 · a true story

The day the Mona Lisa disappeared

Exercise 4 · Reading

The greatest art theft of the 20th century

Read the true story. Then answer — and decide: was Peruggia a clever thief or just very lucky?

Today the Mona Lisa is probably the most famous painting in the world. But in 1911 most people outside the art world had never heard of it. That changed on Monday, 21 August 1911.

That morning the Louvre in Paris was closed to visitors, and the building was almost empty. A small Italian man called Vincenzo Peruggia walked in through the workers' door, wearing the same white coat as the museum staff. Nobody stopped him: he had worked at the Louvre before and had even helped to build the glass case for the Mona Lisa. When the room was empty, he simply lifted the painting off the wall, took it to a staircase, removed the frame and the glass, and left through a service door. A plumber even opened the door for him, because he thought Peruggia was an employee.

Incredibly, nobody noticed until the next day, when an artist arrived to paint a copy and found four empty hooks on the wall. Sixty police officers searched the museum. Even the famous artist Pablo Picasso was questioned!

The police made some big mistakes. Peruggia had left a thumbprint on the glass, and his fingerprints were already in police files — but nobody compared them. A detective even visited his flat and wrote his report on the table — while the Mona Lisa was hidden underneath it.

For more than two years the painting stayed in Peruggia's flat. In 1913 he took it to Florence and tried to sell it to an art dealer. The dealer asked the director of the Uffizi gallery to look at it, and they called the police. Peruggia said he only wanted to bring the painting “home” to Italy. In January 1914, the Mona Lisa went back to Paris — more famous than ever.

03 · як визначити тип

Дивись на дієслова

gets / leaves … + Present → 0
will / 'll → 1
would / 'd + V → 2
would have / had + V3 → 3

Детективна логіка (third): реальність — «собака не гавкав» → умова навпаки: If a stranger had come in, the dog would have barked.
