Part 1 · new grammar

The passive: it was done

Active: Workers founded the club in 1886. — who did it is important. Passive: The club was founded in 1886. — the club is important, not the workers.

be + third form (V3): Present — it is made, they are called · Past — it was built, they were sold. Want to say who did it? Add by: The song was written by the fans.

Perfect for nicknames: They are called the Saints. They are known as the Toffees. Tottenham were named after a knight.

What's in a name?

Exercise 3 · Reading

Factories, birds and toffees

Read about where English club nicknames come from. British words are bold — we'll learn them in the next exercise. Then do the three tasks.

Words to help

found (founded, founded) — заснувати · weapons factory — збройовий завод · cannon — гармата · shipbuilding — суднобудування · steel — сталь · breed (bred, bred) — розводити · weaver — ткач · knight — лицар · medieval — середньовічний · borrow — позичити

In England almost every football club has two names: the official one and the one the supporters actually use. Ask a fan in a pub where he's going on Saturday, and he won't say "to watch Arsenal". He'll say, "I'm off to see the Gunners." So where do these nicknames come from?

Many of them are connected with the jobs people did in the area. Arsenal was founded in 1886 by workers at the Royal Arsenal, a huge weapons factory in south-east London, so the team are still called the Gunners today — and there's a cannon on the club badge. West Ham United started life as Thames Ironworks, a team for the workers of a shipbuilding company. That's why they are known as the Hammers, or the Irons, and why two hammers can be seen on their badge. In Sheffield, a city famous for its steel knives, United are called the Blades. And Stoke City are known as the Potters, because for centuries some of the best plates and cups in Britain were made in Stoke.

Some nicknames come from the colours of the kit. Newcastle United play in black-and-white stripes, like the bird, so they are called the Magpies. Norwich City are the Canaries: they wear yellow and green, and canaries were bred in the city by weavers hundreds of years ago.

Other names have a funnier story. Everton are known as the Toffees. In the club's early days, sweets were sold to fans by toffee shops near the ground, and for many years a "Toffee Lady" has thrown sweets to the crowd before home games. Tottenham Hotspur were named after Harry Hotspur, a medieval knight whose family owned land in the area, so their fans simply call them Spurs. Southampton were started in 1885 by young men from St Mary's Church, and that's why they are still the Saints.

Not every nickname is old. Manchester United were given the name "the Red Devils" in the 1960s — it was borrowed from a rugby team in nearby Salford. Crystal Palace became the Eagles in the 1970s, when their manager was inspired by Benfica from Portugal.

For supporters, the nickname is more than a word. It's sung on the terraces, printed on scarves and shouted across the pitch. When a fan shouts "Come on, you Saints!" or "Up the Canaries!", he isn't talking about a company. He's talking about his town, his family and his history. That's proper football, as they say in England.

A true story

In March 1966, four months before the World Cup in England, the trophy was stolen from an exhibition in London. A week later it was found under a hedge — by a dog called Pickles!

Exercise 6 · Speaking

The name game

Three rounds. Use the passive whenever you can: It's called… · It was named after… · It was founded by… · They're known as…

  1. Guess the club. I give clues in the passive, you guess: “They were founded by church members. They're known for their red-and-white stripes.” Then you make clues for me — for English or Ukrainian clubs.
  2. Ukrainian nicknames. Do you know any nicknames of Ukrainian clubs? Where do they come from? If a club hasn't got one — invent it and explain: “They should be called the … because the city is famous for …”
  3. Your own club. You're starting a new club in your town. What's it called? What are the colours of the kit? What's on the badge? What will the fans be called — and why?

Неправильні дієслова
